Most safety advice sells you one thing: more. More cameras, more alarms, more apps that know where you are. Each one is pitched on what it protects. Almost none is priced on what it takes.

Skif starts from the other end. Before any step is recommended, it is written down with three prices: what it costs in money, what it costs in privacy, and what it costs in freedom.

## One worry, four answers

Take a worry the Quick check offers for a home: people turning up at your door who should not. Here are four of the steps Skif knows for it, priced the way Skif prices them.

| Step, and its price | Privacy | Freedom |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speak through the closed door. **Free** | Untouched | Untouched |
| A door viewer or chain. **Under CHF 100** | Untouched | Untouched |
| A doorbell camera, recording to a card at home. **CHF 100–500** | Records beyond your home | Asks a small routine |
| A cloud camera with face recognition. **Over CHF 500** | Watches people, out of your control | Something else decides |

The last row is the one most shops lead with. It is also the only one that sends the faces at your door, neighbours included, to a company you will never meet.

## The cheapest step first

Skif ranks steps by what they achieve against what they cost, and it shows the free ones first. Of the 63 steps it can recommend today, 35 cost nothing. Most of them are habits, not purchases.

> [!NOTE] Nothing leaves the page
> The Quick check on the home page runs in your browser. What you tick is not sent to Skif.

A camera is sometimes the right answer. So is a person beside you, which is what a Skif Protector is for. But they come after the steps that cost nothing, and you see the price before you choose.

## What is not built yet

The full assessment, which weighs steps against your own limits and budget, needs an account. The prices themselves are Skif's judgement for now, marked as such on each step, and the research pages say which ones the evidence supports and which it does not.
