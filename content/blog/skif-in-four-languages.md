Skif is built in Zürich for people in Switzerland. Until this week it spoke only English. Now it speaks the three languages most people here speak too.

## Pick a language once

The choice sits at the top and the bottom of every page. Skif remembers it, signed in or not, and every link keeps it. An address carries it as well: `/de`, `/fr` and `/it` in front of any page.

## What is translated

| Part of Skif | German, French, Italian |
| --- | --- |
| Menus, footer, sign-in | Yes |
| Emergency numbers and sharing your location | Yes |
| Home page and the Quick check | Yes |
| Act and all 10 checklists | Yes |
| The ten fields of safety | Yes |
| The 15 guides and the research | Not yet |
| This blog | English |

Where a page still holds English, it says so at the top instead of passing off a half translation as a whole one.

## Why four, and not fifteen

A site in fifteen languages is fifteen sites to keep true. Skif is about Switzerland, so the site speaks English and the three national languages most people read.

Protectors are a different matter. A person who stands beside you should speak your language, so the list of languages a Protector can offer is longer: Swiss German and Romansh, Spanish and Portuguese, and Arabic, Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Russian one tap further.

## How the German reads

German on Skif uses *du* and Swiss spelling: *ss*, never *ß*. A test fails the build if an *ß* slips in. French uses *vous*, Italian *tu*.
